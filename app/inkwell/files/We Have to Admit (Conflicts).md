I have to call us out. We’re terrified over the threat caused by this virus after witnessing 13,600 deaths worldwide in three months. I truly believe that many of those 90 year olds with copd and tired hearts would have died anyway. It sounds crass and I mourn if I cause any insult to anyone. But old people are the most susceptible always, to every calamity. I hope one of the key lessons American society can learn from this is more compassion and care to elderly people is called for. I’ve spent time in England, Canada, Germany, as well as the US, and all those countries take better care of their old people than we do. It’s not cool.

A different lesson for all of us is what are we going to do with this resilience we’ve been working so hard on. At least I have, and many people, a lot of them using yoga as a path for staying grounded, using community and circle wisdom from people who have not been lost to greed and blind self-interest.
I looked up today to check, over 100,000 people, vast majority of them civilians, in Yemen have been killed in warfare over the last five years. Has that been on everyone’s radar? How have we not stopped that?
The virus is evidence of a great imbalance we are perpetrating. Good on us for building personal resilience, personal capacity to do good. Now then, what does that look like on the ground, not only during this crisis, whose extent we have to be careful not to imagine worse than it is, but after.
What can we learn, and what can we do better.

I come back to that Foreign Affairs article mentioned in Part One.

If you can produce your own food, if you do not depend on publicly provided electricity or water, you are not only safe from disruptions that may arise in food supply chains or the provision of electricity and water; you are also safer from getting infected, because you do not depend on food prepared by somebody else who may be infected, nor do you need repair people, who may also be infected, to come fix anything at your home. The less you need others, the safer and better off you are. Everything that used to be an advantage in a heavily specialized economy now becomes a disadvantage, and the reverse.

Truth is, it’s a tall order. How can we adapt, and quickly, and not quickly as in urgently, b/c that will slow us down, but as in adroitly, nimbly, creatively, lovingly. Every action we undertake must be scrutinized: is this needed? Does this help, or hinder all Creation?
Indigenous people worldwide have never lost the reverence for everything that underpins all understandings of our common existence here on Earth, with every other living being. That reverence stays our hand, b/c despoiling is not revenant, drone bombing the enemies of our Arabian oil friends is not reverent. We are in over our heads, consuming too much, wanting too much, flailing instead of dancing through life with our souls as headlights upon our common brilliance.
